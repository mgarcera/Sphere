# Sphere — App Store Screenshots

> ## ⚠️ Never shoot this on your own phone
>
> The arc draws **whatever calendar the device is signed into**. On your device that is real
> personal and client data, and a screenshot is permanent and public.
>
> **Shoot only on a simulator that has never been signed into iCloud.** Every event in the frames
> is fabricated, from the seed file in this document. Before any shot, open the menu's
> **CALENDARS** section and switch off everything except the fabricated calendars — the simulator
> ships with `US Holidays`, `Birthdays`, `Found in Mail` and `Found in Natural Language` populated,
> and Sphere will happily draw them as all-day events and capsules.
>
> Check the last item on the [pre-upload checklist](#5-pre-upload-checklist) before every upload.

## 1. The hard requirements

**Upload one set: iPhone 6.9", portrait, 1320 × 2868. That is the whole obligation.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Required** | iPhone **6.9"** only. Everything smaller is generated by scaling this set. |
| **Shoot at** | **1320 × 2868** portrait |
| **Count** | 1 minimum, **10 maximum** per display size. Six recommended below. |
| **Format** | PNG or JPEG, RGB |
| **iPad** | **None.** Sphere is `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1`, iPhone-only. Do not upload an iPad set. |
| **Landscape** | None. The app is portrait-only. |

**The three pixel sizes Apple accepts inside the 6.9" class** — any one of them satisfies the
requirement, so pick one and shoot the whole set at it:

| Pixels | Device | Simulator to use |
|---|---|---|
| **1320 × 2868** | iPhone 17 / 16 Pro Max | **iPhone 17 Pro Max** ← use this |
| 1260 × 2736 | iPhone Air | iPhone Air |
| 1290 × 2796 | iPhone 15 Pro Max, 14 Pro Max, Plus models | — |

**How this was checked.** Apple's own spec page (`developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/
reference/screenshot-specifications/`) is JavaScript-rendered — `curl` returns a shell with none of
the numbers in it, so the canonical table could not be read directly, and a summarizer run against
it returned a garbled row. So each number was verified a second way instead:

- **1320 × 2868 and 1260 × 2736 were measured**, by booting each simulator and running
  `xcrun simctl io <udid> screenshot` then `sips -g pixelWidth -g pixelHeight`. iPhone 17 Pro Max
  emits exactly 1320 × 2868; iPhone Air emits exactly 1260 × 2736. (iPhone 17 Pro emits
  1206 × 2622 — that is the 6.3" class, not 6.9". Don't shoot on it.)
- **1320 × 2868 is known-accepted on this account**: Fil's shipped set in
  `Fil/AppStore/screenshots/` is exactly that size, captured by the same simulator pipeline, and
  Fil is live.
- 6.9"-only-is-sufficient and the 10-screenshot cap are corroborated by Apple's page and by
  multiple current secondary sources.

**UNVERIFIED:** the exact minimum count. Every source says 1, but Apple's page could not be read
directly to confirm it. Irrelevant in practice — six are being shot.

**Alpha channel.** Apple's spec says no alpha. `xcrun simctl io screenshot` writes PNGs that
**do** carry an alpha channel (`sips -g hasAlpha` → `yes`) — and so do Fil's shipped screenshots,
which were accepted. So upload the PNGs as they come. If ASC ever objects, flatten with:

```bash
sips -s format jpeg -s formatOptions 100 in.png --out out.jpg
```

## 2. The frame sequence

Six frames. Apple **OCR-indexes screenshot text**, so captions carry ASO weight as well as
comprehension — keep them in a consistent safe zone and keep the device frame identical across the
set (Fil's convention; match it).

Captions are sentence case, ending in a period, compressed from the shipped description.

| # | Caption | App state to capture | What it has to argue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **See your schedule in the sky's arc.** | Posed date, **13:00**. Clear sky → full blue wash, birds on the arc, black serif type on blue. Time dot **inside** the midday event, so the title reads the event name. Four or five capsules visible across the day. | The whole thesis in one image. |
| **2** | **Close to the sky at any time.** | **Same date, scrubbed to ~22:00.** Whole app cut to dark, night fill under the curve, stars on the high deck, moon-phase disc at the dot. Dot inside the evening event. | Natural Sky — the app *is* the sky. |
| **3** | **Your very own atmosphere.** | **Storm hour.** Three cloud decks forced full and stacking, forked lightning, storm wash darkening the header to near-black. | The weather is real and rendered, not a glyph set. |
| **4** | **Turn the wheel. Move the day.** | Menu → **WHEEL** section: the printed wheel diagram with a position selected, and the PRIMARY / SECONDARY column panel beside it. | No navigation stack; the wheel is the whole interface, and it's reassignable. |
| **5** | **Widgets on your Home Screen and Lock Screen.** | Simulator Home Screen with the small **and** medium `Day` widgets placed, or the Lock Screen with the rectangular one. | Reach outside the app. |
| **6** | **Tuned to where you are.** | Menu, scrolled to show the place name and field, the **TODAY** block with moon + temperature, the `MiniArc` with its sunrise / solar-noon / sunset labels, and the CALENDARS switches. | It's configurable and it's yours. |

### Why frame 1 earns it

It is the only state that shows every load-bearing claim at once — the sun's curve, calendar
capsules riding it in their own colors, the time dot, and the wheel — and it does so in the app's
most saturated, least calendar-app-looking image: a `#4A8CD8` zenith with pure-black serif type
sitting on it. Nothing else in the app looks like any other planner in the category, which is the
job of the first frame.

Two details make it work, and both are easy to get wrong:

- **The dot must sit inside an event.** With no event under it, the title becomes a *live clock*,
  which then disagrees with the 9:41 status bar in the same image. Inside an event the title is the
  event's name and the caption is `Tuesday, July 14 · 11:00 AM – 12:00 PM` — better copy, and the
  clock problem disappears.
- **The blue needs the sun above ~15° and a near-perfect clear reading.** Below that the wash is
  partial and the frame reads washed out rather than deliberate.

**If only two frames are ever seen, they should be 1 and 2** — the same app at noon and at
midnight is the strongest two-image argument Sphere can make, because it is also the feature
nothing else in the category has.

### Deliberately not used

- **A frame for sounds.** The four bells have no visual trace beyond a toggle. Nothing to shoot.
- **Sunrise / midday / sunset marks on the main arc.** `SunMark.swift` is dead code and never
  renders. Those labels exist only on the `MiniArc` inside the menu — which is why frame 6 is where
  they appear.
- **The priming screen.** Good copy, but it only renders on a fresh install before permissions are
  answered, and a first frame that shows a permission wall argues the wrong thing.
- **The EventKit editor.** It's Apple's UI, not Sphere's. Claiming it in prose is enough.

## 3. Capture method

### The one fact that makes this easy

**Every visual state is a function of `model.focusHour` — the hour the wheel is on, not the wall
clock.** The palette, the blue wash, twilight, the night fill, stars, clouds, rain, storms, the
temperature and the condition word all follow the wheel.

```swift
// ContentView.swift
private var skyNight: Bool { nightTurn > 0.5 }          // nightTurn ← focusElevation ← focusHour
private var effectiveScheme: ColorScheme {
    if appearance == .sky { return skyIsNight ? .dark : .light }
    ...
}
```

**You can shoot the full night frame at two in the afternoon.** Do not change the Mac's clock, and
do not wait for sunset. Turn the wheel past the horizon and the whole app cuts to dark — the flip
happens at sun elevation ≈ 0°, over about a two-degree window.

### What the wheel does *not* control

Three things are pinned to the real clock and cannot be posed by scrubbing:

- **The widgets.** `publishSnapshot()` writes today and tomorrow from `.now` — the code comment is
  explicit: *"Everything outside the arc is about NOW, never about the wheel."*
- **The menu's TODAY block** — moon, temperature, and the `MiniArc` — is anchored to real now.

So frames 5 and 6 need the fabricated events sitting on **the real current date**. The seed script
below handles this by writing the same day onto every date you'll shoot.

### The weather window — this will fight you

The sky is a live Open-Meteo fetch. The app asks for `past_days=92, forecast_days=16`, but
**measured today, the API returns nulls for anything older than about 67 days**, and
`WeatherProvider` drops null hours entirely. An hour with no reading draws an **empty sky band**,
which means "not known" — never "clear" — and is the one thing that must not ship in a marketing
frame.

Checked today for Chicago: usable data ran **2026-07-08 → 2026-09-28**. Requested range began
2026-06-13; everything before July 8 came back null.

**Consequence:** you cannot travel back to the June solstice for a taller arc. The earliest usable
date is roughly nine weeks back, and that boundary moves forward every day.

Find the hours you want rather than guessing — this prints the storm hours and the clearest middays
in the currently usable window:

```bash
curl -s "https://api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast?latitude=41.88&longitude=-87.63&hourly=cape,precipitation,cloud_cover_low,cloud_cover_mid,cloud_cover_high&past_days=92&forecast_days=16&timezone=auto" -o /tmp/wx.json
```

```bash
python3 -c "
import json;d=json.load(open('/tmp/wx.json'))['hourly'];t=d['time']
lo,mi,hi,c,p=d['cloud_cover_low'],d['cloud_cover_mid'],d['cloud_cover_high'],d['cape'],d['precipitation']
clr=lambda i: None if lo[i] is None else 1-(lo[i]/100*.7+mi[i]/100*.4+hi[i]/100*.15)
print('LIGHTNING:',[(t[i],c[i],p[i]) for i in range(len(t)) if (c[i] or 0)>=2000 and (p[i] or 0)>0][:8])
print('CLEAREST 13:00:',sorted([(round(clr(i),3),t[i][:10]) for i in range(len(t)) if t[i].endswith('T13:00') and clr(i) is not None],reverse=True)[:8])
"
```

**Today's answer for Chicago**, verified against the live API:

| Frame | Date & hour | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** (hero) and **2** (night) | **2026-07-14**, scrub to **13:00** then **22:00** | Clearness **1.0** at both hours — all three cloud decks at 0% — so the blue is full and the night is starry. Mid-July also gives a taller arc than September. |
| **3** (storm) | **2026-07-17, 18:00** | CAPE **3090**, precipitation **34.9 mm**. Clears both thresholds in `SkyCondition`: `isConvective` (≥1000 + precip) *and* `hasLightning` (≥2000 + precip), so the decks force full **and** bolts draw. |

These dates rot as the window slides. Re-run the command above if you shoot more than a few days
from now.

### Step by step

**Simulator: iPhone 17 Pro Max on iOS 26.5.** Not 27.0 — that runtime is a beta (`24A5355p`) and
you want the set shot on what users are running. Sphere's floor is iOS 26.0, so 26.5 is fine.

**1 — Make a dedicated device, so a dirty run is never expensive.**

```bash
xcrun simctl clone "iPhone 17 Pro Max" "Sphere Shots"
```

**2 — Boot it and take the permission prompts off the table.**

```bash
xcrun simctl boot "Sphere Shots" && xcrun simctl bootstatus "Sphere Shots" -b
```

Granting calendar access up front is what stops `CalendarPriming` rendering — it draws only while
`calendar.access == .undetermined`, and it covers the whole screen.

```bash
xcrun simctl privacy "Sphere Shots" grant calendar com.smidgecraft.Sphere
```

```bash
xcrun simctl privacy "Sphere Shots" grant location com.smidgecraft.Sphere
```

> **UNVERIFIED:** whether `privacy grant calendar` yields EventKit **full** access or write-only.
> Not tested against the app. You'll know within a second of launching: if the priming screen
> appears anyway, the grant didn't take — tap Continue and accept the system prompts by hand, once.
> The cloned device keeps the answer.

**3 — Pin the place.** Sphere falls back to Chicago and labels it `default` in the menu, which you
do not want in frame 6. Set a real fix instead:

```bash
xcrun simctl location "Sphere Shots" set 41.8781,-87.6298
```

**4 — Set the status bar.** 9:41, full bars, charged — the marketing convention, and the same
override Fil's capture script uses.

```bash
xcrun simctl status_bar "Sphere Shots" override --time "9:41" --batteryState charged --batteryLevel 100 --cellularMode active --cellularBars 4 --wifiMode active --wifiBars 3
```

**5 — Set the region to United States**, so the weather line prints °F. The temperature goes
through `.formatted(.measurement(usage: .weather))`, which is locale-driven — a non-US region
renders °C into an American storefront. Settings → General → Language & Region → Region.

**6 — Seed the calendar.** Write the seed file:

```bash
python3 - <<'PY'
import datetime, pathlib
EVENTS = [("Morning run",6,30,7,15),("Studio hours",9,0,10,30),
          ("Design review",11,0,12,0),("Lunch",12,30,13,30),
          ("Hardware store",15,30,16,15),("Dinner",19,30,21,0)]
DATES = [datetime.date.today(), datetime.date(2026,7,14), datetime.date(2026,7,17)]
out = ["BEGIN:VCALENDAR","VERSION:2.0","PRODID:-//Sphere//Shots//EN","CALSCALE:GREGORIAN"]
for d in DATES:
    for n,(t,h1,m1,h2,m2) in enumerate(EVENTS):
        f = lambda h,m: f"{d:%Y%m%d}T{h:02d}{m:02d}00"
        out += ["BEGIN:VEVENT", f"UID:sphere-{d}-{n}@smidgecraft.com",
                f"DTSTAMP:{d:%Y%m%d}T000000Z",
                f"DTSTART;TZID=America/Chicago:{f(h1,m1)}",
                f"DTEND;TZID=America/Chicago:{f(h2,m2)}",
                f"SUMMARY:{t}","END:VEVENT"]
out.append("END:VCALENDAR")
p = pathlib.Path.home()/"Desktop"/"sphere-seed.ics"
p.write_text("\r\n".join(out)); print("wrote", p)
PY
```

Then **drag `sphere-seed.ics` from the Desktop onto the simulator window** and tap **Add All**.

> This step is manual and there is no scripted substitute. Both
> `xcrun simctl openurl <udid> "file://…"` and `open -a Simulator file.ics` were tested here: each
> returned success and **neither imported anything** — the simulator's Calendar database was
> unchanged afterwards. **UNVERIFIED:** that the drag itself imports cleanly; it could not be tested
> without driving the GUI. If it doesn't, add the six events by hand in the simulator's Calendar app
> — it is a one-time cost.

Give the events **two calendars with different colors** (make them in the simulator's Calendar app),
so the capsules come out in more than one color and the description's *"in the color of the calendar
it came from"* is visible rather than asserted.

**7 — Snapshot the seeded device**, so you never do steps 2–6 twice:

```bash
xcrun simctl clone "Sphere Shots" "Sphere Shots CLEAN"
```

**8 — Build, install, launch.**

```bash
xcrun simctl install "Sphere Shots" "$(xcodebuild -project /Users/masongarcera/Documents/GitHub/mgarcera/Sphere/Sphere.xcodeproj -scheme Sphere -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,name=Sphere Shots' -showBuildSettings 2>/dev/null | awk -F' = ' '/ TARGET_BUILD_DIR =/{d=$2} / FULL_PRODUCT_NAME =/{n=$2} END{print d"/"n}')"
```

```bash
xcrun simctl launch "Sphere Shots" com.smidgecraft.Sphere
```

**9 — Pose and shoot.** In the app: switch off every calendar but the two fabricated ones; map a
wheel position to **CAL** in the WHEEL section if you want the date picker (the bottom button
defaults to NOW), then pick the date; turn the wheel to the hour; hold a chevron to step whole days.

```bash
xcrun simctl io "Sphere Shots" screenshot ~/Desktop/sphere-01-hero.png
```

**10 — Frame 5 needs the Home Screen.** Long-press the simulator's home screen → add the **Day**
widget in small and medium. For the Lock Screen variant, **⌘L** locks the simulator — Fil's script
notes this explicitly, correcting an earlier claim that a device was required. It is manual, not
impossible.

**11 — Verify the size of every file before you stop:**

```bash
for f in ~/Desktop/sphere-*.png; do echo -n "$f "; sips -g pixelWidth -g pixelHeight "$f" | tail -2 | tr '\n' ' '; echo; done
```

## 4. Things that will fight you

| Trap | What you'll see | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Simulator's own calendars** | Extra all-day events and capsules you didn't seed — `US Holidays`, `Birthdays`, `Found in Mail` are populated by default. Confirmed present in the simulator's Calendar database. | Switch them off in the menu's CALENDARS section. |
| **Live clock in the title** | Title reads `1:24 PM` while the status bar says 9:41. | Put the dot inside an event. Or match the status bar override to the clock. |
| **Empty sky band** | A bare arc with nothing above it. Means *no forecast*, not clear weather — you scrubbed outside the data window, or the fetch failed. | Stay inside the usable window; confirm the weather line shows a condition word and a temperature. |
| **Date travel loses the sky** | Going back for a taller summer arc silently kills the weather. | ~67 days back is the real floor, not the 92 the app requests. |
| **Widgets ignore the wheel** | Widget shows today's real events, not the posed day. | Seed the fabricated day onto the real current date too — the script does. |
| **Menu's TODAY block ignores the wheel** | Moon, temperature and MiniArc show real now. | Same fix. Frame 6 is a *today* frame. |
| **Blue and night never coexist** | The blue wash is gone by the time the sun reaches 15°; the night cut fires at 0°. | They're separate frames. Don't hunt for one image with both. |
| **Storms suppress everything else** | No blue sky and no sunset behind a storm — the wash suppresses `ClearBlue`, `TwilightBackground` and `NightSky`. | Expected. The storm frame is its own look. |
| **`sips` says hasAlpha: yes** | Apple's spec says no alpha. | Upload anyway — Fil shipped identical files. Flatten only if ASC objects. |
| **Widget palette doesn't follow Natural Sky** | On a light phone at night the app is dark beside a light widget. Known, accepted, recorded in DECISIONS. | Shoot the widget frame in daylight so the mismatch isn't in the picture. |

## 5. Pre-upload checklist

Mechanical only. Each of these has caused a reshoot or a rejection somewhere.

- [ ] Every file is **1320 × 2868**, portrait, PNG. Verified with `sips`, not by eye.
- [ ] **Six files**, all from the **same simulator and same runtime** — no mixed device chrome.
- [ ] Status bar is **9:41**, full cellular and wifi bars, battery charged, on **every** frame.
- [ ] No carrier name, no debug overlay, no Xcode view-debugger tint, no simulator bezel captured.
- [ ] Region is **United States** — the weather line reads **°F**, not °C.
- [ ] Language is **English**, and the set is uploaded to the **en-US** localization.
- [ ] **No real calendar data.** Only the two fabricated calendars are switched on. Read every
      visible event title and confirm you wrote it. No holiday, no birthday, no "Found in Mail".
- [ ] The place name in frame 6 is a real city, not `Chicago · default`.
- [ ] No frame has an **empty sky band** — each shows a condition word and a temperature.
- [ ] The title in every arc frame is an **event name**, not a live clock that contradicts 9:41.
- [ ] Captions sit in a **consistent safe zone** across all six, and the device frame is identical.
- [ ] Caption text matches the shipped description — nothing claims a feature the listing doesn't.
- [ ] No Apple feature name appears in the app **name or subtitle** (the 5.2.5 clause Fil was
      rejected under). "Home Screen" and "Lock Screen" in a *caption* and in the description's prose
      are fine; in the name or subtitle they are not.
- [ ] Screenshots attached to the version in ASC **before** submitting, not after.
